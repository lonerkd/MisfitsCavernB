# Session — 2026-11-01: the deep brain, an independent set, and scripts with no blank lines (B4, first piece)

**Why this and not another weight.** After B5 every cell of the generated corpus reads 0.99+, but the generator and the decoder come from one model of scripts, so that number cannot find what that model is missing. This session wrote an independent set instead: seven scripts by hand (`lib/scriptos/corpus/handwritten/`, about 330 lines) in conventions the generator does not produce, each line labelled **before** the parser was run on it (scene numbers on both sides, V.O./O.S./CONT'D, INTERCUT, montage lists, lowercase and en-dash headings, group cues, a teaser/act structure, a title page). The engine agreed on all but four lines.

**What it found.** One vocabulary gap, `SMASH CUT TO BLACK.` (a list cannot hold every qualified transition; now one pattern covers the cut/dissolve/fade/wipe-to-black-or-white family). Three lines where readers genuinely disagree (`THE END`, `INTERCUT - …`, `INSERT - …`), marked unscored rather than forced. And one real weakness: a script with **no blank lines**, the usual state of text pasted from another editor, read a caps cue after a speech as more speech.

**The fix, chosen by measurement.** `documentProfile` (decode.ts): a script of at least 10 lines with under 5% blank lines is "tight", and there the cost of a cue right after a speech drops. I did not pick the number by eye: the generated corpus with every blank line removed was scored at penalty 14 (before), 4 and 2; 4 won on all three styles. The same measure exposed that non-Latin cues were judged partly by a blank line above them, which a blank-free script never has (INTERNATIONAL cue F1 0.814 → 0.974). Macro-F1 on the new blank-free surface: PLAIN 0.895 → 0.911, REAL_WORLD 0.884 → 0.914, INTERNATIONAL 0.834 → 0.903. Floors for it are in `corpus.test.ts`. Conventional cells did not move (the 0.99 / 0.98 floors held).

**Smaller facts of grammar that came with it:** a caps line with a scene heading or transition directly under it cannot be a cue; a whole-line time card (`TWO HOURS LATER`, `MEANWHILE`, `THE NEXT MORNING`) narrates and ends a speech, while `SEE YOU LATER.` shouted in a speech does not; a line that opens with a known speaker's name and a lowercase word (`Maya ducks.`, not `Maya, wait.`) narrates.

**Caught while tuning:** my first version of the fix (cue penalty 2) broke "action directly before a cue" in the same script, because the decoder had been deciding that case through the cue's penalty. Coupled decisions like that are why the numbers, not the single example, decide.

**Verified:** 12 focused tests (`profile-b4.test.ts`), the hand-written set (7 scripts), the three new floors, and every older floor held; typecheck, lint, 1901 unit tests, build and the bundle budget clean. **Not verified:** any real script (the private corpus is still empty), and the editor in a browser (the change is in the reader, which the editor calls; no UI changed).
