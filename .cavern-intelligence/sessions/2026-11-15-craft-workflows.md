# Session - 2026-11-15: a written workflow for every craft (BACKLOG 3.51)

**The ask:** "are each distinct role designed with their entire toolset and workflow planned or completed?" The honest answer was: toolsets for all 58 crafts, but the step-by-step workflow was written only for 20 roles in a planning document, not as something the suite could show or check.

**Built (no migration):** `lib/roles/workflows.ts`: a workflow per craft as data. Four stages (Prep, The day before, On the day, After the shoot); each step is plain words with the tool it happens in; each workflow says what the role receives and hands on. About 20 crafts have their own (Director, both ADs, Script supervisor, Producer, Line producer and Production manager, Accountant, Coordinator, Location manager, Casting director, DIT, performers, the outside roles); every other craft uses its department's (Writing, Camera, Lighting & grip, Sound, Art, Wardrobe hair & makeup, Editorial & post, Music, Production, Direction), then a generic one. On the Production tab a person with a craft has **Your workflow as <craft>**: their steps in order, each with its tool as a button **only if they can open it** (a gaffer is not offered Money), and what they receive and hand on. It never hides a tool.

**Tests:** unit `workflows.test.ts` (4: every tool named exists; every workflow has real steps, a receives and a hands; every craft in the toolkits resolves; each craft with its own workflow has a step in a tool its suite holds); the integration `role-toolkits.test.ts` gained a check against the real catalogue (all 58 crafts have a workflow with a step in a tool their suite holds, and no workflow names a craft or department that does not exist); browser `e2e/your-workflow.spec.ts` (a gaffer and an accountant see their own steps, tools and hand-offs; the gaffer is not offered Money; clicking a tool opens it; axe clean).

**Not verified:** that each workflow matches how a practitioner really works. They are the engineering's reading of each job from the role completeness map and the tools as built; the practitioner runs (BACKLOG 3.51 item 5) are what correct them. Left (S each): a Your day shortcut to the next step of the day, and an owner-editable workflow per production.
